sections:
  - heading: 'Problem: Drawcord Alone on a Rooftop'
    level: h2
    body: Residential wall mounts often survive with a hem drawcord. Rooftops and open equipment yards see sustained 25+ mph gusts — wind gets under a loose hem and the cover becomes a sail. Bungee cords stretch and detach; the cover ends up on a lower roof or in the yard.
  - heading: 'Solution: Minimum Rooftop Install'
    level: h2
    body: After standard drape and hem secure, add mechanical tie-downs.
    bullets:
      - Ratchet straps or UV-rated cable ties to curb anchors or frame legs — not bungee alone
      - Reinforced top panel if rain or snow load is expected
      - Crown the top slightly with strap tension so water runs to edges, not into louvers
      - Route hem so runoff drains away from the cabinet opening, not into the pan area
  - heading: After the First Storm
    level: h2
    body: Walk the roof and re-tension every strap. Flutter at corners means the cover shifted — fix it before the next front, not after you find it on the ground.
