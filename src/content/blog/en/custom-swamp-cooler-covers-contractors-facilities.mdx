    level: h2
    body: Commercial swamp cooler covers are usually purchased as a project, not as an isolated retail accessory. This guide is for HVAC contractors, facility teams, property managers, and distributors covering several rooftop, side-draft, down-draft, window, or portable units. The goal is to place different cabinet sizes under one controlled specification while keeping each cover identifiable at installation and reorder time.

  - heading: Build an Asset Schedule Before Requesting a Quote
    level: h2
    body: Give every cooler a site or asset ID and measure the outside cabinet rather than the pad or catalog nominal size. Record protrusions, disconnect boxes, pipes, ducts, caster assemblies, control panels, and the side that must remain accessible. Photos of all four sides with a tape measure visible reduce clarification and prevent unlike cabinets from being grouped under one pattern.

  - heading: Combine Mixed Sizes on One Purchase Order
    level: h2
    body: A commercial program does not need every cooler to share one size. Send one schedule with a separate row and quantity for each cabinet pattern. We can review the batch around a shared fabric, color, seam construction, tie-down method, and labeling convention while preserving the individual measurements and openings. This makes approval and future replacement clearer than averaging unlike units into a universal size.
    bullets:
      - Group identical cabinets under one pattern ID and quantity
      - Keep materially different cabinets on separate rows even when nominal model names match
      - Use one approved fabric and color where site conditions allow
      - Put the asset or pattern ID on packing and reorder records
  - heading: Specify Fabric, Fastening, and Logo Requirements
    level: h2
    body: Climate and installation conditions should drive the construction. Tell us whether the covers face desert UV, rooftop wind, rain, salt air, freezing weather, dust, or indoor storage. Then note the required hem, drawstring, straps, zipper access, split corners, vents, or equipment openings. Logo or private-label printing can also be included; print method and practical MOQ depend on artwork, color count, cover material, and quantity.

  - heading: RFQ and Production Workflow
    level: h2
    body: A complete RFQ lets the factory confirm fit, production economics, and freight before an order is approved. Typical production is 10–14 business days after dimensions, materials, quantities, artwork, and pattern details are approved. Transit time is quoted separately, and large or complex programs may require additional production time.
    bullets:
      - 1. Send the asset schedule, quantities, unit type, photos, destination, and required date
      - 2. Receive a technical review with material options, confirmed MOQ, lead time, and quote
      - 3. Approve dimensions, opening layout, artwork, and any qualifying pre-production sample
      - 4. Production begins after order and specification approval
      - 5. Retain the approved pattern IDs for simpler repeat orders when the equipment has not changed

  - heading: What to Include in Your First Message
    level: h2
    body: Send enough information to distinguish a purchasing inquiry from a generic price request. The most useful first message includes company and project name, cooler type, quantity by size, outside dimensions, site photos, climate or fabric requirement, logo file if needed, delivery country and postal code, and the date the covers are required. If measurement is still in progress, send the unit list first and identify which fields remain open.
