sections:
  - heading: Covering Is the Last Winterizing Step
    level: h2
    body: A cover blocks wind, dust, precipitation, and pests, but it cannot prevent water left in the cooler from freezing. Shut off power and water, drain and clean the unit, let every wet surface dry, seal the indoor duct where applicable, and install the cover only after those jobs are complete.
    bullets:
      - Follow the cooler manufacturer's shutdown instructions before removing panels or pads
      - Never service the cooler while it is energized
      - Do not cover wet pads, a wet sump, or water-filled distribution lines
      - Use a cover profile that matches the unit instead of forcing a generic tarp around ducts and brackets
  - heading: Tools and Information to Prepare
    level: h2
    body: Gather an adjustable wrench, screwdriver, bucket, soft brush, dry towels, pipe insulation, and the cleaning product approved by the cooler manufacturer. Before ordering a cover, record the model, maximum outside width × depth × height, mounting type, and every pipe, electrical box, bracket, or duct that changes the cabinet outline.

  - heading: Core Shutdown Steps for Every Cooler
    level: h2
    body: Complete the work in this order so moisture is not trapped behind a freshly installed cover.
    bullets:
      - Disconnect power at the plug or service disconnect and verify the unit cannot start
      - Close the water supply, disconnect the feed where required, and drain the tank or sump completely
      - Remove debris and mineral buildup with methods approved for the pad and cabinet material
      - Drain pumps and small distribution tubes; use low-pressure air only when the manufacturer permits it
      - Air-dry the open unit fully, then reinstall dry components or store removable pads indoors
      - Insulate exposed pipes and seal the interior duct opening with a damper or insulated panel
  - heading: Instructions by Cooler Type
    level: h2
    body: The preparation sequence is shared, but storage, openings, and tie-down locations change with the installation.
    dataTable:
      headers: [Cooler type, Winter action, Cover detail]
      rows:
        - [Portable, Drain the removable tank and pump; store indoors when possible, Use a breathable dust cover indoors or a fitted weather cover outdoors]
        - [Rooftop or fixed, Drain the sump and water line; seal the indoor duct, Reinforced top and secure tie-downs with clearance for pipes and motor housing]
        - [Wall-mounted, Inspect brackets and protect exposed lines, Close fit around wall brackets with multiple wind straps]
        - [Window-mounted, Remove and store indoors when practical; otherwise seal frame gaps, Fitted outer cover plus an insulated interior panel]
  - heading: Fit and Secure the Winter Cover
    level: h2
    body: Slide the dry cover over the cabinet without dragging it across sharp corners. Align custom openings with pipes, ducts, and brackets, pull the hem below the lowest intake louver, and tighten the drawcord or straps evenly. The cover should be secure without crushing panels or becoming airtight; designed vents or controlled drainage help condensation escape.

  - heading: Winter Checks and Spring Start-Up
    level: h2
    body: After storms, remove heavy snow and check that straps remain tensioned and water is not pooling on top. In spring, remove and dry the cover before storage, inspect pipes and the basin for freeze damage, reinstall serviceable pads, reconnect water, check for leaks, and restore power only after the cooler is ready to run.
  - heading: Quick Questions
    level: h2
    body: A plastic tarp can provide temporary protection, but loose folds catch wind and can trap condensation. Draining remains necessary even under a fitted cover. The cover should be weather-resistant and secure, not hermetically sealed. If the unit has unusual ducts, brackets, or control boxes, send measurements and photos for a made-to-measure pattern.
