  - heading: The Short Answer
    level: h2
    body: A cooler stored in a restaurant, shop, storeroom, or warehouse usually needs a breathable fitted dust cover. A cooler left on a patio, terrace, rooftop, farm, or job site needs a weather-resistant, UV-stable cover with stronger fastening. In both cases, drain and dry the unit first.

  - heading: Inside Covers for Restaurants and Indoor Spaces
    level: h2
    body: Portable coolers used in restaurants, cafés, shisha lounges, shops, gyms, event halls, and workshops collect fine dust, grease, and insects while idle. A fitted cover also looks cleaner than a sheet in a customer-facing area and avoids fabric dragging around chairs or caster wheels.
    bullets:
      - Choose breathable fabric, but still empty and dry the sump and pads before covering
      - Use a fitted cut with an elastic or drawstring hem for frequent access
      - Select a washable surface where kitchen grease or workshop dust is expected
      - Match color or add a logo when the covered unit remains visible to customers
  - heading: Outside Covers for Patios and Job Sites
    level: h2
    body: 'Outdoor portable coolers face rain, freezing water, UV, wind, sand, and dust. Specify the cover as a system: coated fabric, drainage, ventilation where needed, reinforced corners, and fastening that cannot lift off the hem. A loose tarp leaves intake gaps and wears through at cabinet edges.'
    bullets:
      - Use UV-stabilized weather-resistant fabric for prolonged exposure
      - Add screened vents when the site needs condensation control
      - Use a drawstring plus straps or buckles in wind
      - Confirm whether casters and handles remain accessible while covered

  - heading: Choose for the Climate and Storage Condition
    level: h2
    body: Southwest US sites usually prioritize UV, dust, and wind; wet European winters prioritize rain, damp, and frost; Gulf and Middle East sites add extreme sun and sand; North African coastal sites combine UV with seasonal rain. The same region can contain very different sites, so send the exact location, whether the unit is sheltered, and how long it remains unused.

  - heading: If the Cooler Moves In and Out
    level: h2
    body: Choose for where the cooler spends most of its unused time. If it remains outside overnight, specify an outside cover. If it is stored indoors for the whole off-season, a lighter inside cover is usually enough. For long periods in both places, use an outside-grade cover with appropriate ventilation so it can protect outdoors without trapping residual moisture indoors.
  - heading: Measure the Portable Cooler
    level: h2
    body: Portable coolers do not follow one universal cabinet size. Measure maximum outside width, depth, and height; include casters only if the cover should extend toward the floor; and record handles, control panels, water inlets, louvers, and hose connections. Send the model and photos as supporting information, but the actual measured cabinet controls the pattern.

  - heading: Prepare the Cooler Before Covering
    level: h2
    body: Disconnect power, drain the tank and sump, clean pads and internal surfaces, and let the unit dry completely. A cover protects a prepared cooler; it does not correct water left inside. Install the fitted cover last and secure every closure.

  - heading: Common Questions
    level: h2
    body: An outside cover can be used indoors when it has suitable ventilation, although it is heavier than necessary. An inside dust cover should not be used outdoors because it is not designed for rain, strong UV, or wind. We make unit covers for restaurant and café coolers, but not interior ceiling registers. A close-fitting outside cover reduces sand entry; it cannot create a sealed enclosure around an operating cooler.

Choose an inside cover for dry indoor storage and an outside cover for weather exposure. The correct fabric, ventilation, and tie-downs depend on the cooler being fully drained and on where it spends its unused time.
