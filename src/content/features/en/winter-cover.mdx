  - Insulated thermal barrier (foam or quilted lining)

  - Snow-shedding top reduces load on flat cabinets

faqs:
  - q: Is a winter cover the same as insulated?
    a: Winter covers include insulation plus weather sealing for cold seasons. Insulated covers may be specified without full snow-shed top geometry.
  - q: Do I still need to drain the cooler?
    a: Yes. Draining and drying are mandatory. The cover protects against exterior freeze and debris, not standing water inside.
  - q: Can I use a winter cover in a mild climate?
    a: You can, but a lighter water-resistant cover may suffice. Winter SKUs are optimized for freeze and snow regions.
  - q: When should the winter cover come off?
    a: Remove at spring commissioning before the first cooling run. Inspect pads and pan, then store the cover dry.

sections:
  - heading: Why Winterize With a Cover
    body: Freeze damage often starts in residual moisture in the pan and distribution system. A winter cover does not replace draining — it adds a layer against ice, wind-blown snow, and debris after you complete shutdown procedures.
    bullets:
      - Blocks wind-driven snow into louvers
      - Reduces thermal shock on exterior panels
      - Keeps rodents and nesting debris out of the cabinet

  - heading: Insulation & Fabric Options
    body: Winter SKUs bond 3–5 mm foam or quilted batting to a 600D shell. Outer face is water-resistant; inner face is treated against mildew. Extreme cold sites can specify thicker cores on custom orders.
    bullets:
      - 'Standard winter line: 600D + foam core'
      - Upgrade to waterproof shell for wet snow regions
      - Charcoal and gray stocks; custom colors on volume
      - Heavier than summer covers — plan for two-person rooftop lift
  - heading: Installation Checklist
    body: Shut down power, drain water, dry internals, then install the cover with all closures engaged. Route tie-downs so they do not compress insulation unevenly or tear corner stitching.
    bullets:
      - Never cover a running unit
      - Remove cover before spring startup inspection
      - Store folded loosely in summer — do not compress foam long term

Winter protection combines insulation, a snow-shedding top profile, and perimeter sealing to slow freeze-thaw damage on pads, float valves, and sheet-metal cabinets when the cooler is offline for months.
