  - heading: Tools Needed
    body: A steel tape measure (preferred over cloth), notepad or phone camera, and optionally a helper for large rooftop units. For down-draft coolers, use a ladder with fall protection and measure from the roof deck to the top of the cabinet.
    bullets:
      - Tape measure (ft/in or metric)
      - Camera — photos speed up custom quotes
      - Ladder and harness for rooftop units
      - Model nameplate photo (optional but helpful)
  - heading: 'Step 1: Measure Width'
    body: Width is the longest horizontal side of the cabinet footprint, usually the left-to-right dimension when facing the access panel. Measure at the widest point including corner trim — not across the pad opening inside the hood.
    bullets:
      - Measure twice at mid-height and near the top in case the cabinet tapers
      - Include side flanges or motor humps that extend past the main box
      - Record in inches to the nearest ¼ in. (or mm)
  - heading: 'Step 2: Measure Depth'
    body: Depth is the front-to-back dimension of the exterior cabinet. On side-draft units mounted to a wall, depth is from the wall surface to the outermost point of the cooler housing (not the pad frame only).
    bullets:
      - Account for water lines, float valves, and brackets that project beyond the cabinet
      - If a pipe exits the side, mark its location for a custom opening
      - 'Wall-mount units: measure from the wall to the farthest protrusion'
  - heading: 'Step 3: Measure Height'
    body: Height runs from the mounting surface (roof curb or wall bracket base) to the highest point of the cabinet — often a vent cap or lift lid. For down-draft units, include the rain collar if it projects above the roof line.
    bullets:
      - Do not compress the measurement if a spring-loaded hood rises above the frame
      - 'Flat-top units: add note if water pools — we can specify drainage grommets'
      - 'Round units: measure height on the straight side, then diameter separately'
  - heading: 'Step 4: Note Protrusions'
    body: 'List every item that breaks the rectangular outline: electrical disconnect boxes, side ducts, overflow drains, service panels, and guy-wire anchors. Custom openings or split corners prevent stretched fabric and torn seams.'
    bullets:
      - Mark disconnect box location (common on side-draft commercial units)
      - Photograph all four sides — one missing side causes fit issues
      - Specify if the cover must clear a locked access door
      - See common sizing mistakes before you submit your quote request
---

Accurate measurements prevent gaps that let in dust, wind, and rain. Measure the outside of the metal or plastic cabinet — not the pad, not the internal frame. Record width, depth, and height at the widest points, then note any obstructions that need cutouts or extra slack.
