  - heading: Polyester (300D–900D Oxford)
    body: 'Polyester is the most common cooler-cover fabric because it balances cost, weight, and weather resistance. Denier (D) indicates thread thickness: 300D is lighter and suited to sheltered installs; 600D is the industry workhorse for residential and light commercial units; 900D canvas-style polyester handles large spans and rooftop exposure.'
    bullets:
      - 'PU (polyurethane) coating: water-resistant, good for most dry and mixed climates'
      - 'UV stabilizers: essential for rooftop and desert sun — pair with our UV-shield products for direct exposure'
      - 'Typical weight: 0.8–2.0 kg depending on size and denier'
      - 'Best for: side-draft, down-draft, and standard custom covers where you need durability without vinyl stiffness'
  - heading: Canvas
    body: Canvas covers use a heavier woven base (often cotton-poly blend or polyester canvas) with a rugged hand-feel. They resist abrasion and punctures better than light polyester, which matters on industrial sites and rooftop units with sharp flashing or gravel.
    bullets:
      - Heavier than 600D polyester — plan for stronger tie-downs on large covers
      - Often paired with PU or wax-style treatments for water resistance (not fully waterproof without lamination)
      - Popular on commercial and extra-large cooler covers
      - Allow extra drying time after rain before storage to prevent mildew on natural-fiber blends
  - heading: Vinyl & PVC-Backed Fabrics
    body: Vinyl and PVC-backed polyester deliver the highest rain protection. Sealed seams and a waterproof rating (e.g. 5000 mm hydrostatic head) keep water out of pads and cabinets during off-season storage. These fabrics are stiffer and louder in wind than oxford polyester.
    bullets:
      - Ideal for heavy-rain regions and units left covered through wet seasons
      - PVC backing adds stiffness — specify split corners or zippers if you need access to disconnect boxes
      - Anti-mildew treatments recommended for humid climates
      - See our waterproof premium cover line for fully sealed construction
  - heading: Mesh
    body: High-density polyester mesh blocks leaves, dust, and pests while allowing airflow. Mesh is not a winter cover — use it in-season or in mild climates where condensation under a solid cover is a concern.
    bullets:
      - 'Typical mesh aperture: ~1 mm for debris filtration without starving airflow'
      - Ultra-light (0.3–0.6 kg) — easy on/off during operating season
      - 'Pairs well with solid winter covers: mesh in summer, insulated or solid cover in winter'
      - Browse mesh cooler covers for breathable protection options
  - heading: Insulated Materials
    body: Insulated covers add a foam or thermal lining (commonly 3–5 mm) to reduce freeze risk, block drafts, and slow temperature swing inside the cabinet. They are thicker and need slightly larger clearances than uninsulated covers.
    bullets:
      - Recommended for winter storage and cold-climate off-season protection
      - 'Condensation management: ensure the unit is dry before covering; vented hem designs help'
      - Heavier than standard polyester — use reinforced corners on rooftop units
      - Compare insulated vs. mesh vs. solid covers in our material comparison blog

Fabric choice drives waterproofing, UV resistance, weight, and lifespan. Most swamp cooler and evaporative cooler covers use coated polyester (300D–900D), canvas, vinyl-backed fabrics, breathable mesh, or insulated laminates. Use this guide to match material to how and where your unit runs.
